How parts talk to your board

A sensor has to tell the board what it measured, and the board has to tell a light or a fan what to do. There are a few ways to do this. In the Seed cards you use only the first three. Try each one below. It takes about five minutes.

Used in these cards

1. Digital: on or off

The simplest message has two values. The pin is either HIGH (5 volts) or LOW (0 volts), with nothing in between.

Your code uses it to switch things: digitalWrite(pin, HIGH) turns a pin on.

In the cards: the LED (Card 1), the fan (Card 3) and the buzzer (Card 4).

Pin 9 is LOW (0 V)
The line shows the pin over time.
Used in these cards

2. Analog: a voltage that changes smoothly

Many sensors answer with a voltage that rises and falls with what they measure. An analog input reads how high that voltage is and gives your code a number from 0 to 1023.

Your code reads it with analogRead(pin).

In the cards: the temperature sensor on pin A0 (Cards 2, 4 and 5).

2.50 V → your code gets 512
A digital pin would only see: HIGH
Used in these cards

3. Serial: text, one bit at a time

The board sends text to your computer through the USB cable. Each letter becomes a row of bits, and the bits travel one after the other. This way of sending is called serial, or UART.

Both sides must agree on the speed, called the baud rate: 9600 baud means 9600 bits per second. If the board sends at one speed and the computer listens at another, the text turns into nonsense.

Your code sets the speed with Serial.begin(9600) and sends a line with Serial.println("LED ON"). The small lights TX and RX on the board flicker while data moves.

In the cards: "Messages from the board", in every card.

Other ways you will meet later

Many sensors use these. You do not need them for the Seed cards, so here are only their names and one line each.

  • I2CSeveral sensors share two wires, and each sensor has its own address. Common for humidity sensors and small displays. On the UNO the two wires are the pins marked SDA and SCL.
  • SPIA faster link with four wires, used for memory cards and some displays.
  • 1-WireOne data wire for many sensors, used by some soil-temperature probes.
  • SDI-12The standard for field sensors that growers and researchers bury in soil, such as soil-moisture probes.
